# Research: where power comes from on the FE 1.5T

Ticket: `.scratch/downpipe-power/issues/01-power-fundamentals.md` · researched 5 Oct 2026 · scope: Civic FE 1.5T (L15 family, DI, mono-scroll turbo with electric wastegate, CVT), VN E10 RON95, KTuner Starter 21.

Confidence: **high** = engineering or maker text plus an independent second source agree · **medium** = two sources agree but one is a vendor or a forum tuner, or the FE detail is inferred from the 10th gen · **low** = plausible inference, flagged as one. Vendor claims are marked **(vendor)**. Civic X (FC/FK) evidence is cross-check only and never a card number.

## Answer

"More air, more fire, more fuel?" Only the first one makes power. The other two either follow it or are capped.
1. **Air makes torque.** Torque scales with the mass of air trapped in each cylinder per cycle. Fuel is metered to that air at a fixed lambda, and spark only sets *when* it burns. Boost (psi) is a pressure, not a flow: the same psi with less exhaust backpressure traps more air (a downpipe does this). The size of that gain on the FE is open, for tickets 02 and 05.
2. **Fuel follows air. It is not a power lever.** Best power on a boosted engine sits near λ0.82–0.85. This map asks λ0.75 (11.0 AFR) on purpose: the extra fuel cools the charge against knock and keeps turbine-inlet heat below its limit (production engines go to about λ0.73). More fuel than that adds no power. Going leaner chases a few percent on paper and gives up knock and heat margin, on RON95 E10 in Vietnamese heat. Wrong for the street.
3. **Fire is capped by knock.** Spark makes the most torque at MBT. Under boost on pump fuel the engine knocks before MBT, so the ECU sets timing from knock and octane. E10 RON95 is about US 89–91 AKI. More boost on knock-limited fuel forces timing out, so you get the same power with more heat.
4. **The ECU decides how much air is allowed.** Honda's ECU is torque-based: pedal → torque request → boost and throttle, capped by torque limits (protecting the CVT) and by Cylinder Fill Limitation. A downpipe makes the same air cheaper to get. It does not raise those caps, and KTuner does not expose the torque limiter.
5. **Response and lag** come from exhaust energy, which depends on rpm and turbine pressure ratio. A downpipe lowers turbine outlet pressure, so it can bring boost in sooner (direction: medium confidence; size unknown). The CVT keeps rpm low at part throttle, which is also the LSPI zone (about 1,500–2,500 rpm at high load), so keep low-rpm boost down.

## Contradictions and nuances with current cards

- **X1 · `kc-expectations`**: "so more boost means more heat, not more power." Sound on the stock exhaust: wastegate about 2.5% open at boost means no turbine headroom (A5, C2). After a downpipe, though, turbine discharge pressure falls, so some wastegate headroom and VE come back at the same psi (A4, A5, E2). The card needs a downpipe scope line, not a reversal. Second point: the card states "The map adds up to 58 lb-ft, or 79 Nm" with no label. That is a KTuner claim ("up to 58 lb-ft", S20) and should read as one.
- **X2 · `kc-table-boost`**: "Cylinder Fill Limitation caps air per cylinder". True, but tuner sources describe it as a knock-retard-indexed safeguard that closes the throttle (D3). The everyday cap on boost is the torque limiter (D2), which KTuner does not expose (D4). `e10-ignition-boost-simple-tune.md` calls the fill table "the real limiter", which is not backed by the sources here.
- **X3 · `kc-rich-wot`**: "Richer than asked is the safe side, not a fault". Agreed for knock and heat. Not stated: running past λ0.73 is richer than the production enrichment limit and costs a little power (B3, B4). This is wording, not a safety error.
- **X4 · `kc-cvt`**: "no torque limit is used for this car." This means the app uses no torque number. But the ECU does enforce torque limits that protect the CVT and can pull boost (D1, D2). As written, the card can be read as "nothing limits torque". Say "the app sets no torque number; the ECU's own limiter stays in charge".
- No contradiction found in `kc-tune-order`, `kc-open-loop-wot` or `kc-ignition-formula`. The sources support all three (B5, C1, C4).
- Repo notes, not cards: the "E10 RON95 ≈ US 91" in `e10-ignition-boost-simple-tune.md` §1 should read ≈ 89–91 AKI (C3).
